# Research notes (verified from retrieved sources; Nov 2026)
## WORLD — Russia / Stavropol (Trukhmen)
- Russia 2021 census: 41,328 Turkmens (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens infobox). Tajikistan 15,171 (2010), Belarus 5,231 (2019), Pakistan 171,000, Turkey 113,762 (2024), Uzbekistan 194,076 (2026).
- Trukhmens (Stavropol krai): migrated into the Caucasus in the 17th c. (Tokarev via Red Book EKI https://www.eki.ee/books/redbook/trukhmens.shtml); ~15,000 in 1958 (Tokarev); early-19th-c. Russian sources call ALL Turkmens "Trukhmens".
- Trukhmen = dialect of Turkmen; 9,357 native speakers in Stavropol (2021) (en.wikipedia Trukhmen); contact with Nogai, Tatars, Kalmyks.
- Turkmensky District (Stavropol Krai), est. 1925, centre Letnyaya Stavka; 2010 census pop 25,948, Turkmen 5,043 = 19.44% (not a majority) (en.wikipedia Turkmensky_District).
- Nomadic with Kalmyks in 19th c. ("Trukhmen steppe") (kaspy.asu-edu.ru 2023 article); Chur village majority Turkmen, Sunni Hanafi.
- DO NOT USE llmpedia (LLM-generated).
## WORLD — Salars (China)
- Salar people: Turkic minority in China, language Salar = Oghuz branch; 165,159 (2020 census, en.wikipedia Salar_people); 130,607 (2010; inalco.fr/en/salar, ~87,000 in Xunhua per unofficial data).
- Tradition & Chinese chronicles: descendants of the Salur tribe (Oghuz); folk etymology of "Salur": "those who wield swords, hammers, spears" (factsanddetails/mugtama; folk, not scholarly).
- Legend: brothers Haraman & Ahman (Karaman/Akhiman) fled Samarkand (13th–14th c.), ~18 companions (some versions 170 families), Quran on camel -> Xunhua, Qinghai; the Quran kept in Jiezi Mosque, Xunhua. "Camel dance" retells the journey.
- Linguistics: Oghuz origin possible but influenced by Chagatai, NW & SE Turkic; ~20% Chinese + ~10% Tibetan vocabulary in Amdo (en.wikipedia Salar_language); Ili Salar ~4,000 (Xinjiang).
- Regions: Xunhua Salar Autonomous County, Hualong (Qinghai), Jishishan (Gansu), Ili (Xinjiang). Sunni Islam.
- Scholarly caution: origins "uncertain" (Ma & Stuart, asianethnology.org); intermarriage with Tibetans, Hui, Han.
## WORLD — Pakistan / Turkey
- Pakistan: "over 60,000 people of Turkmen ethnicity ... according to both UN and national estimates" (en.wikipedia Turkmen_in_Pakistan) — BUT the Turkmens infobox says 171,000 => CONFLICT; show range 60,000–171,000 (low confidence). Mostly refugees: fled Turkmenistan -> Afghanistan after 1917 Bolshevik revolution, then Afghanistan -> Pakistan after the 1979 Soviet–Afghan war (RFE/RL a/1096306; Wikipedia). 2005 census: >6,000 Afghan Turkmen in Balochistan alone (0.8% of local Afghans). Carpet weaving = main livelihood; Ersari refugee school at Attock (Barakat, 1994). Some Pakistani Turkmen (Peshawar, Lahore carpet traders) shifting to Turkey (Marsden, Univ. Copenhagen page).
- Turkey: Migration Directorate data to 23 Mar 2023: 113,449 citizens of Turkmenistan hold residence permits (3rd after Russia; short-term 86,188); 217,669 permits of all kinds issued to Turkmen citizens (Turkmenistan Helsinki Foundation, tmhelsinki.org/en/article/7c68626f...). RFE/RL (a/32630102): ~198,000 Turkmen citizens legal as of mid-September (2023); Turkey ended visa-free travel for Turkmen in Sept 2022 at Ashgabat's request; real number thought much higher. (HRW-sourced claim ">1 million" in atalayar.com — weak, not used as fact.)
- Afghan Turkmen in Istanbul: bulk arrived en masse in 1982 after Pakistan exile; community leader Abdulkerim Mahdum persuaded Turkey to grant refugee status (IWPR iwpr.net/tysbemd3). Their ancestors crossed from today's Turkmenistan in early 1920s.

## GENEALOGY — meanings of the 24 Oghuz tribe names
Source: Eda Durmuş, "Oğuz Boy Adlarının Anlamları" (Pamukkale Üniv. SBE Dergisi 69, 2025; DOI 10.30794/pausbed.1631033; https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/journal-file/34254), summarising Kashgari (Divan, 11th c.), Rashid al-Din (Oghuznama, 14th c.; Togan 1982), Yazıcıoğlu (15th c.), Abu'l-Ghazi (Shajara-i Tarakima, 17th c.) + modern etymology of Ercilasun 2008 ("Oğuz boy adlarının etimolojisi", Dil Araştırmaları 3).
General: Kashgari lists 22 tribes (no Kızık, Karkın; Yaparlı appears as "Çaruklug" per Sümer); Kızık+Karkın = two Khalaj tribes that joined the Oghuz (Sümer 1999). Name meanings are virtues: bravery, endurance, agility, wealth, humility, obedience etc. Each tribe had its own summer/winter pastures and its own brand-mark (tamga). Tribe=boy (Old Turkic bod); bodun=tribal union; il=polity.

## GENEALOGY — modern Turkmen tribes (source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_tribes, fetched; cites Abu'l-Ghazi, Edgar 2006, Peyrouse 2015, Stuart, Poullada 2018, Ataniyazov 1994/1999)
- Major modern tribes: Teke, Yomut, Ersari, Chowdur, Goklen, Saryk (Peyrouse 2015; Edgar 2006); most numerous = Teke. Five göl on flag/arms belong to these tribes.
- All traced to the 24 Oghuz; Abu'l-Ghazi (Shajara-i Tarakima, 1659, text pp.207–218) gives the Salur special emphasis: Teke, Yomut, Ersari derived from Salur. Salur leader "Salur Ogurcik Alp" had six sons: Berdi, Buka, Usar, Kusar, Yaycı, Dingli (Shajara p.214).
- Dynasties linked to early Oghuz tribes (belief/tradition): Seljuks←Qiniq(Kınık), Khwarazmians←Begdili, Qara Qoyunlu←Yiwa(Yıva), Aq Qoyunlu←Bayandur, Ottomans←Kayi, Afsharids←Afshar (Wikipedia; Safa 1986 Camb. Hist. Iran; Roxburgh 2005).
- TEKE ("billy goat" in Turkmen): descended from Salur 11th–12th c.; two parts Ahal Teke & Mary Teke (Merv). Stuart (1830s observations): four clans Wakil(Wekil), Beg, Suchmuz, Bukshi; Wakil+Beg = "Toghtamish" (descended from a person of that name); Suchmuz+Bukshi = "Otamish". Stuart 1881: Akhal Tekke 25,000 tents; Merv Tekke 40,000 tents incl. Salor 5,000; 5 persons/tent => ~325,000. >1.6 million today (2014 est.; over a third of Turkmenistan's population). Resisted Persia; Gökdepe 1879 Turkmen victory over Russians, 1880–81 second battle, Merv taken 1884; commander Ovezmurat Dykma-Serdar commissioned major in Russian army. Ahal and Mary Teke separated by Persian-controlled Deregez zone. Ahal/Mary Teke dialects are basis of standard Turkmen (Clark).
- ERSARI/Ärsary: "er" brave man/master + "sary" light/yellow (Wikipedia gloss; folk etymology). Four sub-divisions: Kara, Bekewul, Gunesh, Uludepe. Major part of the "Sayin Khan" Turkmen confederacy (13th–17th c.), yurt from Balkan mountains to Mangyshlak and north to the Emba; divided Ichki (inner) / Tashki (outer) Oghuz (Abu'l-Ghazi doesn't say whether organizational, military or geographical). 17th c.: after the western Uzboy channel dried they moved east to the Amu Darya; sub-tribe Ali-Eli stayed near Kaka (Ahal). Live in Turkmenistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan.
- SARYK: mostly on the upper Murghab (Olson et al. 1994).
- CHOWDUR (Çowdur): direct descendants of Chavuldur; tradition: left flank of Oghuz Khan's army; E Caspian shore; in Mangyshlak since 11th c. (Abu'l-Ghazi); 1221 Mongol conquest pushed Oghuz from Syr Darya into Karakum/Caspian; early 16th c. an aymaq of Sayin Khan confederation; after Kalmyks entered Mangyshlak they ended SE of Khiva under Yomut authority; some mid-Amu Darya near Charjui; 19th c. Khiva: Chowdur incl. Igdir, Bozachi, Abdal, Arabachi (Poullada 2018).
- YOMUT: descend from Salur; Balkan velayat south (Etrek–Gorgan) & Dashoguz; sedentary (Chekishler, Esenguly) / semi-nomadic / nomadic (majority); last de facto ruler of Khiva Junaid Khan was Yomut (Keller 2001). 1873 Khiva campaign: von Kaufman ordered Golovachev to "annihilate the Turkmen Yomut" (Asiatische Studien 2006 p.459).
- Tribal structure vocabulary (Ataniyazov 1999): halk, il, taýpa, urug, kök, kowum, kabile, aýmak/oýmak, oba, bölük, bölüm, gandüşer, küde, depe, desse, lakam, top, birata, topar, tire. Leaders serdar; elders aksakal (white beards) chosen mostly by consensus; unwritten customary law töre/adat; decide water/land/war.
- Mikhailov (1900): "all Turkmen, rich and poor, live almost completely alike".

## GENEALOGY — Yomut / Salor / misc (search batch 3)
- YOMUT (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomut; article flagged "may need rewriting" Oct 2024; cites Mikhailov 1900, Karpov 1925): three main lineages (urug): Jafarbay (Cäfärbay), Aq (Ak) and Atabay; Karpov 1925: Aq+Atabay merged => "Ak-Atabay", old names persist as sub-divisions. Divisions today: Gorgan/Gurgan Yomut (Iran: Gorgan plain & S. Caspian), Balkan Yomuts (W, Shagadam), Dashoguz Yomuts/Khiva Yomut = Bayram-Shali (W of Khiva, N Turkmenistan). Marefa: Sheref (6 subdivisions), Chony (10), Bayram-Shali (5), Kujfik-Tatar (8) [weaker source].
   Traditions: "Yomut descended from the concubine of Salor-Kazan"; "mother of the Teke was an Iranian khansha" (Necef & Annaberdiyev 2003 via biblio.kz article on Teke–Yomut relations 18–19 c.). Kushtdepdi rite (UNESCO 2017). Yomut loose coalition of autonomous clans each with own khan; known for two-sided carpets; headdress names vary by tribe: börük (Teke), topby (Sarakhs), bagmak (Ersari), chanie (Nokhur), uramak (N. Yomut), aladaŋy (W. Yomut), kars (Khatab, Mukry), dastar (Salyr).
- SALOR (countrystudies.us/turkmenistan/2.htm): "Salor confederation" on Mangyshlak and around Balkan mountains; one of few original Oghuz tribes to survive; late 17th c. union dissolved, three senior tribes moved east then south: Khorazm (Amu Darya delta), middle Amu Darya, Akhal oasis & Kopet Dag along Iran border, Murgap river. Salor groups also in Turkey, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, China (cf. Salars). Wikipedia: Salyr declined after military defeat before the modern period.
- Caucasian Knot: until the 1930s Turkmen kept tribal division with multi-step clan structure; biggest: Teke, Yomut, Ersari, Salir, Sarik, Goklen, Chovdur. Dialects: Teke, Sarik, Goklen, Yomud, Karadashli-Alili, Ersari; Anaus, Khasar, Nokhur, Chovdur; Charjou.
- Iran: Yomut, Goklen, Teke live in Iran (Wikipedia Iranian Turkmens); Igdir, Saryk, Salar also since 16th c.
- Wikipedia (Teke/Turkmen tribes cavac copy): Merv Teke "included Imir, Dukur, Düker (Döger), Igdir, Chavuldur, Karkin, Salor or Agar tribes" — NEEDS CONTEXT CHECK (fetch en.wikipedia Teke_(tribe)).
- Bilkent thesis: "A historical and semantical study of Turkmens and Turkmen tribes" (Öztürk? 2008, LCC DK855.5.T85 O93 2008) — repository.bilkent.edu.tr/handle/11693/15366.

## GENEALOGY — Teke details (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_(tribe), fetched in full)
- Oghuz forebears of the Teke migrated to Transoxiana in the 8th c. (Wikipedia). Burnes (Travels into Bokhara, 1834) calls them "Turka". Stuart: in the 1830s the Teke began to settle in the lower Murghab delta near Merv (which they destroyed c.1855); from there extended to Kizil-Arvat => Ahal Teke (between Kizil-Arvat and Gäwers; Stuart: "Daman-i-Kuh") and Merv (Mary) Teke (mainly between Tejen and Murghab). O'Donovan 1881: Merv Turkomans only 26 years in the oasis (since c.1855); earlier around Sarakhs on the upper Tejen, pushed out by the Persians. Rawlinson 1879: Akhal Teke "an integral portion" of Merv Teke, brought from "Labab" (Lebap), banks of the Oxus; "Akhal" borrowed from one of their chief obahs (camps); the oasis strip 2–16 miles wide, ~160 miles from Kizil-Arvat to Gawars; ~8,000 tents = 40,000 souls (avg of estimates). Russian officers 1879: Ahal Teke "30,000 yurts", 50,000 armed men (20,000 cavalry); main body between Geok-Tepe and Askhabad; Anau and Gäwers further east. Ahal and Mary Teke separated by Persian-controlled Deregez (Dargaz) zone.
- Teke carpet göl = guşly göl ("least variation of all Turkoman guls", O'Bannon), top rosette on the national flag; its layout reproduced in Altyn Asyr bazaar. Each tribe historically had its own carpet pattern, clothing, headgear, dialect.
- Language (Clark 1998): Teke dialect has two subdialects: Ahal (Tejen & Ashgabat districts to Gizilarbat incl. Büzmeyin, Gökdepe, Bäherden, Bami, Goch) and Mari (Mari, Türkmengala, Sakarchäge, Murgap, Bayramali). The standard language is based on Ahal & Mary Teke dialects; standard Turkmen is "closest to the Teke dialect, esp. Ahal subdialect" because many 1930s language specialists were Ahal Teke. Early-1920s–30s attempts at a mixed all-dialect language failed after purges (Edgar 2006). Soviet policy diminished tribal identities, which remain socially important.
- Etymology (Stuart 1881): "Tekke means wild goat; also the old he-goat that leads a flock"; modern dictionary: "billy goat" (Frank & Touch-Werner 1999).
- NOT VERIFIED/NOT USED: the sentence "Imir, Dukur, Düker (Döger), Igdir, Chavuldur, Karkin, Salor, Agar tribes" (context unknown).

## GENEALOGY — Ovlat / kinship (search batch 4)
- Kinship (everyculture.com Turkmens-Kinship): segmentary system of territorial descent groups, patrilineal, tribes subdivided into smaller groups; common ancestor Oghuz Khan; oral genealogies; strangers ask each other's descent group on meeting; households not closely related camping together in one oba often discover a tenuous tie; marriage doesn't link descent groups much.
- Owlad/övlat "holy lineages" (SAGE Encyclopedia of Global Religion, "Turkmenistan"; daftareroshan blog quoting scholarly article; countrystudies.us/turkmenistan/14.htm; Wikipedia Islam in Turkmenistan): traditionally SIX övlat in order of perceived holiness: Khoja, Seyit, Shïkh, Magtïm, Ata, Müjewür; each traces descent, via Sufi missionaries, to one of the first four caliphs (not to Oghuz Khan); revered, give blessings/healing, attend life-cycle events, mediate between clans & tribes; ethnographers see them as a revitalised ancestor cult infused with Sufism; "six are active"; strictly endogamous per everyculture (which says five lineages — sources differ 5 vs 6); live dispersed among all tribes, in small compact groups since 18–19th c.; some pre-Soviet Russian scholars labelled them "non-Turkmen" along with tribes of local Iranian or pre-Oghuz Turkic origin that don't trace to Oghuz Khan.
- Founders' shrines (AYAN Turkmenistan travel): Magtym Meazzem, Dana-ata, Gyzly-ata, Pakyr-shikh — shrines of founders of the saintly groups (ovlyat); ≥700 sacred places in the country.
- Islam spread to Turkmen mostly via Sufi shaykhs (countrystudies): adopted as "patron saints" of clans -> "founders".

## GENEALOGY — Göklen (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, "GÖKLEN", Mustafa Aydın, 2020, islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/goklen — summarised, not quoted)
- Live in NE Iran (Gorgan, "Türkmen Sahra"), Turkmenistan (Karakala), Uzbekistan (Ilyali–Türtkul near Khiva). Possibly related to the Turkmen "Gök" people who in the 11th c. moved from the Syr Darya to the Balkan area (W Turkmenistan) and later returned. After 16th c. water shortage, Turkmen tribes (Salur, Ersari, Yomut...) moved to the watered lands of S Turkmenistan; Göklen settled between the Etrek and Gorgan rivers (Akiner).
- Name: no firm explanation; suffix -len/-leng appears in N Iranian tribe names (Bakhtiari Heftleng, Cheharleng) => "Göklen" probably "abode of the Göks".
- Divisions: Kırık(Kınık), Bayındır, Yenkak, Senkrik, Gerkez(Çerkez), Çakır-Beydili, Arap, Ay-Derviş, Kara-Balkan, Erkekli, Kayı, Şeyh Hoca (Haentzsche; Vámbéry; İA) — groups of Oghuz tribes (Kınık, Bayındır, Beğdili, Kayı) inside them are decisive for Göklen formation.
- Raiding & slave sale to Khiva (like Teke, Yomut); Agha Muhammad Shah Qajar (d.1797) sent troops, many killed/captured; some accepted Persian rule; transition to settled farming (Burnes). 1834 raid on Gorgan -> fled under Khiva's Allahqoli Khan; in 1836 Persian attack only 2,000 of 9,000 families remained (Marvin); Persia took 1,000 guards; silk, rice, wheat, millet; heavy sheep tax (Khiva 12,000 sheep + 1,200 for court under Sufyan Khan 1529–35, more than Teke+Yomut+Saryk combined 8,000).
- Population: Fraser 1821–22: 8,000–15,000 families (~10,000 = 50,000 people); Burnes: 9,000 families (45,000); Bode 1837: 12,000 families (~60,000); Vámbéry 1863: 12–15,000 families (up to 75,000); Soviet 1926–27: 55,900 in Turkmen SSR (Ilyali 38,000 + Karakala 17,900); Iran: ~90,000 (Ay-Derviş, Güllüdağ, Cergelan, Yomut highlands); TDV estimate: ~300,000 today (estimate).
- Earlier-settled => quieter life & higher culture; many studied in Iranian & Turkestan madrasas => literature. MAGTYMGULY belongs to the GERKEZ clan of the Göklen; father Döwletmämmet Azady; nephew-pupil poet Zelili; poet Seydi (Ersari by origin, raised among Göklen).
- Iranica "Guklān": confederacy of Gorgan region (NE Iran), Qara Qal'a district (Turkmenistan), Khiva region (Uzbekistan); moved to SW Khwarazm early 16th c. due to water shortage (Akiner p.315).
- Caucasian Knot: smaller Turkmen tribes include Yazirs (Karadashli), Yemreli, Bayats ...; Turkmen of Iraq & Syria "Turkomans" = separate; Wikipedia: Turkmen tribes' westward migration 11th–18th c. contributed to formation of Anatolian Turks, Iraqi & Syrian Turkmens, Turkic population of Iran & Azerbaijan.
- Notable khans/serdars respected: Aba Serdar, Keýmir Kör, Nurberdi Han, Gowshut Han, Dykma Serdar (Wikipedia Turkmen tribes).

## GENEALOGY — Shajara-i Tarākima (en.wikipedia Shajara-i_Tarākima & Abu_al-Ghazi_Bahadur; Britannica)
- Abu'l-Ghazi Bahadur Khan: b. 24 Aug 1603 Urgench, Khan of Khiva 1643/44–1663 (d. 1663/64); descendant of Genghis Khan via Arab Shah; spent ~10 years in Persia; wrote in Khiva-dialect Chagatai. Works: Shajara-i Tarākima ("Genealogy of the Turkmens") completed 1659 (some sources 1661) and Shajara-i Turk (1665, completed by his son Anusha Muhammad Bahadur who commissioned Mahmud b. Mulla Muhammad Zaman Urgench). Western edition: Desmaisons, Histoire des Mogols et des Tatares, St Petersburg 1871–74; first Kazan publication 1825; Turkish version by Vefik Ahmed Pasha, Kazan 1864; Kononov 1958 Russian ed. "Rodoslovnaya turkmen".
- Written "at the request of the Turkmen mullahs, sheikhs and begs", who thought earlier Oghuznamas full of "errors and differences". Three parts: Quranic info (Adam...), Oghuz–Turkmen epic (Oghuz Khan & descendants), info from oral sources. Roughly follows Rashid al-Din's Islamized/Mongolized version of Oghuz Khan's origin.
- Oghuz Khan: six sons, each with 4 sons by first wives (the 24 grandsons = ancestors of the 24 main tribes, heads of aymaks) and more by other wives (other tribes joined the main 24). Two wings: BOZOKS ("Gray Arrows") = Gün, Ay, Yıldız lines; ÜÇOKS ("Three Arrows") = Gök, Dağ, Deniz lines. Bayandur: founders of Ak Koyunlu (and Kara Koyunlu per this list); Yıva: Qara Qoyunlu & Oghuz Yabgu state; Kınık: Seljuk Empire.
- Recent scholarly paper: Kahya 2024, "The ethnocultural and sociological analysis of migrations documented in Shajara-i Tarākima (Turkmen genealogy)", Humanities & Social Sciences Communications 11 (Nature).

## LANGUAGE (search batch 5; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_language, /Turkmen_grammar, /Turkmen_alphabet; webonary.org/turkmen; inalco.fr/en/turkmenistan-country-and-language)
- Oghuz (SW) Turkic. Speaker numbers conflict across snapshots: native 4.7M in Turkmenistan + ~0.36M NE Iran (one version); 6.56M (2009–15); 6.1M (2021); total 7.8M incl. L2 1M (2021–23). Ethnic Turkmens in Turkmenistan 5.1M (2020, Wikipedia). => present "about 6–8 million (estimates differ)".
- Official language of Turkmenistan; recognised minority language in Afghanistan. Native in Turkmenistan, Russia, Iran, Uzbekistan, Afghanistan, Tajikistan.
- Dialects (12): Teke, Nohurly, Ýomud, Änewli, Hasarly, Nerezim, Gökleň, Salyr, Saryk, Ärsary, Çowdur (+Trukhmen in Stavropol). Written/standard Turkmen based on Teke. In Iran most speakers use Yomud; in Afghanistan Ersary prevails. Teke sometimes called "Chagatai" in Afghanistan but all Turkmen dialects show only limited influence of classical Chagatai.
- Teke "lisp": /s/,/z/ realised as interdental [θ ð] (shared with Bashkir); Yomut and Ärsary also, less strongly (webonary).
- Phonology: 9 vowels (a, e, ä, i, y, o, ö, u, ü) + 21 consonants = 30 letters; phonemic short/long vowels (length not marked in orthography except üý and ä); vowel harmony front/back; native words are entirely front or entirely back; two verb types: -mak (back) and -mek (front); loanwords (Russian, Persian, Arabic) don't follow harmony; suffixes follow last vowel (kitap > kitabyndan). Consonants coloured by vowels: kaka (back velars) vs köke (front velars). Stress usually on the last syllable. Native words don't end in rounded vowels -> "suw" (Turkish "su").
- Typology: agglutinative, no grammatical gender, SOV, 6 cases, evidentiality marked. Examples: obalardan "from the villages" vs itlerden "from the dogs"; suw/su 'water'; asman (Persian loan)/gök 'sky'; dogry/doğru; numbers bir, iki, üç, on, ýigrim 20, otuz 30, kyrk 40.
- Scripts: Perso-Arabic until 1920s; Turkmen SSR reform 1923–29 (modified Arabic); Latin "Jaꞑalif" 1928/29–1940; Cyrillic 1940–1993; Latin again: decree approved by Mejlis 12 April 1993 (new alphabet version published Feb 1993); revised version current since 1999. 30 letters. Iran & Afghanistan still use a modified Arabic script; a modern standardised Turkmen Arabic alphabet by Dr. Atagazly (booklet "Türkmen Ýazuw Kadalary").
- Letters: A B Ç D E Ä F G H I J Ž K L M N Ň O Ö P R S Ş T U Ü W Y Ý Z (30).

## CARPETS (search batch 6)
- UNESCO RL: "Traditional Turkmen carpet making art in Turkmenistan", inscribed 2019 (14.COM, Bogotá, decision 12 Dec 2019), reference 01486 (ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-turkmen-carpet-making-art-in-turkmenistan-01486). Element = whole chain: Saryja sheep breeding, wool shearing/processing, spinners, dyers, loom & tool makers, designers, weavers, restoration masters; "integrated into social & cultural life; sign of cultural identity and unity". Law of Turkmenistan "On applied arts" (2000); bodies: Ministry of Culture, State Association "Turkmenhaly", State Institute of Culture, carpet enterprises in each velayat. Communities incl. districts Tagtabazar, Yoloten etc. Items listed in the inventory: ensi? (door rug), asmalyk, chuval (bag), khorjun (saddle-bag, for food & small things), "ilkselik" (spindle bag), torba.
- Everyday forms (encyclocraftsapr.com/carpet-making, Turkmen heritage site): ensi (door curtain of the yurt), gapalyk (adornment of the yurt entrance), termech (ornament within), at haly (horse rug), ichirgi (saddlecloth), asmalyk (wedding embellishment of the camel), torba (bags replacing cupboards), hordjun (travel bag), large bag.
- Wikipedia Turkmen rug: Teke design often called "Bukhara" in trade; Ersari main carpet = octagonal "elephant's foot" gul; most famous tribal rugs: Yomut, Ersari, Saryk, Salor, Teke; Turkmen Carpet Museum in Ashgabat; five traditional carpet motifs (guls) on the red band of the flag/emblem represent the five major tribes — traditional order Teke, Yomut, Arsary, Chowdur, Saryk; State association Turkmenhaly working since 1923 (Wikipedia).
- Salor: once the most powerful; after decline its gul was borne by others (khinkhwab blog — secondary).

## SARYK (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saryk; /Panjdeh_incident; Lansdell 1885; Britannica "Turkmen tribes and Russian invasion")
- Etymology (suggested): Middle Turkic saryγ "yellow" or Kipchak saryq "sheep". Early 19th c. lived in the Merv region; from 1830 driven further up the Murghab valley by the Teke; Bala Murghab and Panjdeh became main settlements. 1881 under Russian control after Geok Tepe and creation of the Transcaspian Oblast; 1885 population estimated 65,000 (Lansdell). Regions: Murghab valley (Turkmenistan, Iran, Afghanistan); Turkmen (Saryq dialect), Dari; Sunni.
- Carpets: dark red-brown with the pattern in fine thin lines; symmetric (Turkish) knot like the Yomut.
- Panjdeh incident (Feb–30 Mar 1885): armed engagement Emirate of Afghanistan vs Russian Empire at Panjdeh/Kushka (today Serhetabat); ~600 Afghan soldiers vs >2,500 Russians; Anglo-Russian crisis; defined NW border of Afghanistan. Serhetabat (ex Gushgy/Kushka) renamed 29 Dec 1999.
- A German-language ethnographic text (digital.blb-karlsruhe.de) places the Saryk at Yoloten and Panjdeh; scattered between Saryk and Merv Teke live the former owners of Panjdeh, the Salor.

## CLOTHING & JEWELLERY (search batch 7)
- Iranica "CLOTHING xxvi. Clothing and jewelry of the Turkmen" (Peter Alford Andrews, 15 Dec 1992; https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/clothing-xxvi/): until the 1970s the most elaborate tribal costume still used in Persia. Men until the 1920s: tight-sleeved robe (dōn) of striped silk over loose white cotton trousers (balaq) and a calico shirt (köynek), caught at the waist by a sash (qušaq), with a cylindrical black (sheepskin) hat. Women: principal garment is a shift (köynek), formerly silk, raspberry red common; embroidery differs slightly in colour range and motifs among Yomut, Gökleŋ, Teke and Ērsārı; most fully developed on the mantle with vestigial sleeves (čırpı) still worn by Teke women, draped from the crown of the tall formal headdress; a full-sleeved version is called kürte; colour signifies age and status. Yomut version = bürenğek (simpler, green silk with red). Teke metalwork: fretted, overlaid with parcel gilt within sinuous chased outlines, set with carnelians; Yomut: stronger contour, small lozenges or lunettes of gilt repoussé.
- Jewellery (Google Arts & Culture "Traditional Turkmen Women's Jewelry of the Teke Tribe"; Museum of Asia and Pacific, Warsaw exhibition "The Turkmens: the art of everyday life"; MFAH "Thread & Ornament"; Met Museum): sewn onto the costume and worn over it, covering headgear, neck and hands; women adorned from childhood to old age. Pieces: asyk (heart-shaped, "hanging"; joins braid pendants; amulet protecting women from illness esp. around pregnancy), sulsuleh (forehead), chckeliki (temples), bukau (ceremonial necklace), bilezik (bracelets), tumar (amulet), guliaka (circular pendants). Silver believed to enhance fertility, so younger women wore more. Fire-gilt silver, repoussé, table-cut carnelian and turquoise. Jewellery were amulets more than decoration. Horses also adorned.
- Telpek: large black or white sheepskin hat (Wikipedia Culture of Turkmenistan); varieties (Buhar telpek tall, Şipir telpek short) — only in a weak source (ecieco) → not used.
- Tribal differences (weak source ecieco): Saryk finer patterns; Ersary darker colours simpler patterns — not used as fact.

## CUISINE (search batch 8; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_cuisine; whats4eats; factsanddetails/advantour)
- Wikipedia: similar to rest of Central Asia; typical dishes: tamdyrlama, ichlekli, yarma, dograma, different kinds of pilaf, çorba, somsa, pishme, süzme, çal, agaran. Fried: somsa, gutap (often spinach), fitchi. Pomegranate (nar) as flavouring in oases, seeds crushed for juice; pumpkin seeds crushed for cooking oil. Tomatoes — a New-World import — became a major influence in the last two centuries. Bread: thick round disc-shaped loaves baked in a clay tamdyr oven; "highly impolite to turn a loaf of bread upside down or to mistreat bread". Plov (palaw) served at celebrations and to honoured guests; one source calls it "king in Turkmen cuisine": rice with mutton, carrots, onions, garlic in a big cast-iron kazan. Shurpa (meat broth) = base of most soups; most traditional soups = various types of unash (milk + noodles, or beans + noodles). Dograma (dogramaly şurpa) = diced organ meats + bread, onion, tomato; dumpling soup etli börek şurpasy; mung-bean soup şurpa maş. Pishme served by girls at Novruz with dried fruits. Sorghum porridge etli köje. Çal = fermented camel milk (Wikipedia list).
- whats4eats: shared heritage with Uzbekistan/Kazakhstan; influenced by Iran, Afghanistan, Indian subcontinent; nomadic imprint: lots of meat, bread, dumplings, dairy, little vegetables; dograma "sometimes considered the national dish".
- Işlekli: shepherds' meat pie traditionally baked buried in hot sand and embers (chefspencil, secondary, matches existing app text).

## BOOKS — Dede Korkut (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Dede_Korkut; Uzuntaş 2022 Anadolu ve Balkan Araştırmaları Dergisi; Anikeeva 2019; Jirmunskiy 2016 dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/tdded/article/252621)
- Dresden manuscript (first known; found by H. O. Fleischer in the Dresden Royal Library): intro + 12 stories; title "Kitāb-ı Dedem Ḳorḳud Alā Lisān-ı Tāife-i Oġuzān" ("The Book of my Grandfather Korkut in the language of the Oghuz tribe"). Vatican manuscript: intro + 6 stories ("Hikāyet-ı Oġuznāme-ı Kazan Beġ ve Gayrı"). Then the 22-page Ankara and 61-page "Günbed" (Gonbad) manuscripts, and the Bursa manuscript. Gonbad manuscript = found in a private collection in Gonbad-e Kavus, Iran, 2018; its title: "Cild-i Duyyum-i Kitāb-i Türkmän (ä)lsānî" = "Second Volume of the Book of the Turkmens".
- 13th chapter = sayings attributed to Dede Korkut. Oral tradition long before being written down, 14th–15th c. (Aq Qoyunlu era) per most; stories reflect Oghuz struggles; "only medieval epic of the Turkic peoples that remained in written form" (Anikeeva).
- UNESCO: "Dede Qorqud/Korkyt Ata/Dede Korkut Heritage: Epic, Tale and Music" inscribed 2018 (13th session; ref 01399) on the Representative List, submitted by AZERBAIJAN, KAZAKHSTAN and TURKEY (not Turkmenistan). Wikipedia: heritage of Oghuz-origin peoples, mainly Azerbaijan, Turkey, Turkmenistan. Turkmen name Kitaby Dädem Gorkut; Gorkut Ata statue in Ashgabat.
- Terms: from mid-10th c. the name Oghuz was gradually replaced by "Turkoman"; process completed by early 13th c.; Turkomans = Turks (mostly Oghuz) who had embraced Islam and become more sedentary. 14th c.: Aq Qoyunlu federation.

## UNESCO WORLD HERITAGE in Turkmenistan (en.wikipedia List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Turkmenistan; whc.unesco.org)
- Three inscribed sites: State Historical and Cultural Park "Ancient Merv" (1999; Mary; id 886), Kunya-Urgench (2005; Daşoguz; id 1199; criteria ii, iii), Parthian Fortresses of Nisa (2007; Ahal; id 1242; criteria ii, iii). Wikipedia lists "Silk Roads: Zarafshan-Karakum Corridor" and "Cold winter deserts of Turan" under the TENTATIVE list (2023) — advantour claims they're inscribed (not trusted). Merv = first site (1999). Turkmenistan UNESCO member since 1993; National Commission 1997.
- Merv: 30 km east of Mary; >4,000 years of history (ayan travel, weak); Seljuk capital in 11th–12th c.; Sultan Ahmad Sanjar r.1118–1157, mausoleum in Sultan Kala, early 12th c.
- Kunya-Urgench: capital of Khwarezm; Kutlug Timur minaret ~60 m (sources 60–62 m; "tallest in Central Asia"), 18 decorative bands + 3 Kufic bands naming Kutlug; Turabek Khanum mausoleum (14th c.; ~1370 per one source), Turabek = Uzbek Khan's daughter & Kutlug Timur's wife; Sultan Tekesh mausoleum (late 12th c.); Il-Arslan; Najm ad-Din Kubra (killed by Mongols 1221); city destroyed by Genghis Khan 1221.
- Nisa: Old & New Nisa; "one of the earliest and most important cities of the Parthian Empire" (mid-3rd c. BCE to early 3rd c. CE).
